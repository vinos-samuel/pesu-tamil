## 1. `saapdu-register` — eat (peer/cousin) vs. eat (elder, respectful)
Members: *saapdu* ("Eat"), *saapidunga* ("Please eat — respectful")

1. Your cousin is playing next to you at the table. What do you say to get them eating? → **Eat** / Please eat (respectful)
2. Paati made your favorite dish and you want her to eat too. What do you say? → Eat / **Please eat (respectful)**
3. Thatha is sitting down for dinner. What do you say to him? → Eat / **Please eat (respectful)**

## 2. `vaa-register` — come (peer/cousin) vs. come (elder, respectful)
Members: *inga vaa* ("Come here"), *vaanga* ("Come — respectful")

1. You want your little cousin to come play with you. What do you say? → **Come here** / Come (respectful)
2. Thatha just arrived at the door. What do you say to welcome him in? → Come here / **Come (respectful)**
3. You're calling your friend over to see something cool. What do you say? → **Come here** / Come (respectful)

## 3. `negative-imperative` — the "-aadhey" ("don't") pattern
Members: *satham podaadhey* ("Be quiet"), *azhaadhey* ("Don't cry")

1. Your baby cousin is crying and you want to comfort them. What do you say? → Be quiet / **Don't cry**
2. Everyone's banging pots and it's too loud. What do you say? → **Be quiet** / Don't cry
3. Savyr is upset about a lost toy and starting to tear up. What do you say to comfort him? → Be quiet / **Don't cry**

## 4. `hunger-polarity` — hungry vs. not hungry
Members: *pasikkudhu* ("I'm hungry"), *pasika illa* ("Not hungry")

1. It's dinner time and your tummy is rumbling. What do you say? → **I'm hungry** / Not hungry
2. You already had a big snack and don't want dinner yet. What do you say? → I'm hungry / **Not hungry**
3. Amma asks if you want a snack right after lunch. What do you say? → I'm hungry / **Not hungry**

## 5. `quantity` — enough / a little more / a lot
Members: *pothum* ("I'm full/enough"), *innum konjam* ("A little more, please"), *neraya* ("A lot")

1. You're stuffed and can't eat another bite. What do you say? → **I'm full/enough** / A little more / A lot
2. The food's really good and you want just a bit more, not a whole plate. What do you say? → I'm full/enough / **A little more** / A lot
3. Someone asks how much rice you want, and you're starving. What do you say? → I'm full/enough / A little more / **A lot**

## 6. `go-question-form` — let's go vs. shall we go?
Members: *povom* ("Let's go"), *povoma?* ("Shall we go?")

1. Everyone's ready and it's time to leave — you're telling them, not asking. What do you say? → **Let's go** / Shall we go?
2. You're not sure if everyone's ready yet, so you're checking. What do you say? → Let's go / **Shall we go?**

## 7. `eat-question-form` — eat vs. did you eat?
Members: *saapdu* ("Eat"), *saaptiya?* ("Did you eat?")

1. You want your cousin to start eating. What do you say? → **Eat** / Did you eat?
2. You're checking whether your cousin already had dinner. What do you say? → Eat / **Did you eat?**

## 8. `come-go-motion` — come here vs. go
Members: *inga vaa* ("Come here"), *po* ("Go")

1. You want your dog to come to you. What do you say? → **Come here** / Go
2. You're telling your cousin to go get something from the other room. What do you say? → Come here / **Go**

## 9. `wake-up-pair` — wake up vs. get up
Members: *enthiri* ("Wake up"), *yenthi* ("Get up")

*(Near-synonyms — worth checking with a native speaker whether these situations actually land differently, or if they're close enough that this cluster isn't worth testing. Flagging rather than guessing.)*

1. It's morning and your cousin is still asleep. What do you say to wake them? → **Wake up** / Get up
2. Your cousin is awake but still lying down, and you want them up and moving. What do you say? → Wake up / **Get up**

## 10. `want-polarity` — want to eat vs. don't want
Members: *saapadu vaenum* ("I want to eat"), *vaendam* ("Don't want")

1. You're hungry and ready for food. What do you say? → **I want to eat** / Don't want
2. Someone offers you more food but you've had enough. What do you say? → I want to eat / **Don't want**
