# v1 Phrase List (Units 1–5)

> Supersedes the unit sequencing in `curriculum.md` for v1 scope. Draft, partially native-reviewed as of 2026-08-09 (Vinos has corrected several items directly — see below); still needs a full pass before final sign-off. Romanized Tamil is for production/authoring reference only, **never shown on screen to the learner**.

**Audio policy (locked 2026-08-09):** Units 1, 3, 5 get real native-speaker recordings (kept in `VoiceNotes/`, one file per phrase). **Units 2 and 4 do not need audio** — kinship terms and numbers are simple/common enough to teach as English-spelled Tamil pronunciation text instead (e.g. "amma" shown with a pronunciation cue, no audio file). The `phrases.needs_audio` column reflects this per-item.

**Contrast/callout policy (locked 2026-08-09):** wherever a phrase has a natural opposite, negative, question form, or commonly-confused pair, call it out in the phrase's `notes` — statement, e.g. "opposite of X" or "question form of Y." This is authoring-side scaffolding only; in-app copy still never uses grammar terminology (no "this is a negation suffix") — the pairing itself, taught through usage and repetition, is the lesson. Savyr and future learners here are old enough for this kind of pattern-noticing (same instinct that helps with Mandarin, etc.) — we're just feeding it via contrast, not lecture.

## Unit 1 — Greetings & Courtesy (11 — audio ✅ all recorded)
1. Hello — *vanakkam*
2. How are you? (peer/cousin) — *eppadi irukka?*
3. How are you? (elder — Paati/Thatha) — *eppadi irukkeenga?* (honorific — own item, not a variant)
4. I'm fine — *nalla irukken*
5. Yes — *aamaa*
6. No — *illa*
7. Thank you — *nandri* — commonly said in English even at home; Tamil optional, either is fine
8. Sorry — *sorry / mannikkanum* (recorded — register as used; register question resolved by recording)
9. Goodbye — *poyttu varen* (lit. "I'll go and come" — not word-for-word)
10. Good night — *iravu vanakkam* — commonly said in English even at home; Tamil optional, either is fine
11. Good morning — *kaalai vanakkam* ⚠️ not yet recorded, not yet native-reviewed — commonly said in English even at home; Tamil optional, either is fine

## Unit 2 — Family & Kinship (14 — no audio needed, text-pronunciation only)
1. Mom — *amma*
2. Dad — *appa*
3. Grandma — *paati* — also used for grandma's/grandpa's own sisters (grand-aunts), not just direct grandmother
4. Grandpa — *thatha* — also used for grandma's/grandpa's own brothers (grand-uncles), not just direct grandfather
5. Older brother — *anna*
6. Older sister — *akka*
7. Younger brother — *thambi*
8. Younger sister — *thangachi*
9. Mom's brother (bonus) — *mama*
10. Dad's sister (bonus) — *athai* — elder/younger split for Dad's sisters still deferred to v1.1 (uncle-side split now covered below)
11. Dad's elder brother (bonus) — *periyappa*
12. Dad's elder brother's wife (bonus) — *periyamma*
13. Dad's younger brother (bonus) — *chitappa*
14. Dad's younger brother's wife (bonus) — *chitti*

## Unit 3 — Mealtime Words (11 — audio: 8 ✅ recorded, 3 ⚠️ new, not yet recorded)
1. I'm hungry — *pasikkudhu* — opposite: item 9, *pasika illa*
2. I want water — *thanni vaenum*
3. Give me water — *thanni kodu* ⚠️ direct, not "please pass"
4. I want to eat — *saapadu vaenum* (corrected from earlier draft "saapida vaenum" — Vinos has recorded this as `saapadu-vaenum`)
5. Tastes good! — *rusiyaa irukku* — *tastiya irukku* is also fine, commonly used as an alternative
6. Is it spicy? — *kaaramaa irukka?* — note the *-a* question form vs. item 5's plain *-u* statement form (*irukku* = "it is" / *irukka* = "is it?") — same pattern shows up again in Unit 5
7. I'm full / enough — *pothum* — contrast: item 8 *innum konjam* (want a little more), item 11 *neraya* (a lot)
8. A little more, please — *innum konjam*
9. Not hungry (bonus, contrast) — *pasika illa* ⚠️ not yet recorded — opposite of item 1, *pasikkudhu*
10. Don't want (bonus, contrast) — *vaendam* ⚠️ not yet recorded — opposite of *vaenum* (items 2, 4)
11. A lot / a lot more (bonus, contrast) — *neraya* ⚠️ not yet recorded — opposite of *konjam* (item 8)

## Unit 4 — Numbers 1–10 (10 — no audio needed, text-pronunciation only)
*onnu, rendu, moonu, naalu, anju, aaru, ezhu, ettu, onbadhu, pathu* — no register flags, stable across dialects.

## Unit 5 — Action Commands & Routines (16 — audio: 10 ✅ recorded, 6 ⚠️ new, not yet recorded)
1. Come here — *inga vaa* — contrast: item 11, *po* (go)
2. Sit down — *ukaaru* (corrected from earlier draft "utkaar" — Vinos has recorded this as `ukaaru`)
3. Eat — *saapdu* — question form: item 14, *saaptiya?* (did you eat?)
4. Wake up — *enthiri* (corrected from earlier draft "ezhundhiru" — Vinos has recorded this as `enthiri`) — contrast: item 12, *yenthi* ⚠️ not yet recorded (spelling/meaning confirmed by Vinos, native speaker)
5. Sleep — *thoongu*
6. Wash your hands — *kai kazhuvu*
7. Brush your teeth — *teeth brush pannu* (locked: Tanglish form, not the formal *pல் thei*)
8. Let's go — *povom* — question form: item 13, *povoma?* (shall we go?)
9. Wait — *nillu* — related: item 11 *po* (go), item 15 *nada* (walk)
10. Be quiet — *satham podaadhey*
11. Go (bonus, contrast) — *po* ⚠️ not yet recorded — opposite of item 1, *vaa*
12. Get up (bonus, contrast) — *yenthi* ⚠️ not yet recorded — contrast with item 4, *enthiri*
13. Shall we go? (bonus, question form) — *povoma?* ⚠️ not yet recorded — question form of item 8, *povom*
14. Did you eat? (bonus, question form) — *saaptiya?* ⚠️ not yet recorded — question form of item 3, *saapdu*
15. Walk (bonus) — *nada* ⚠️ not yet recorded — related to item 9, *nillu*, and item 11, *po*
16. Where are you going? (bonus, sentence-level) — *yenga pora?* ⚠️ not yet recorded — first full-sentence item, previews the Unit 6+ direction (see below)

## Appendix — Recognize-tier distractor pattern

For any Recognize screen, the 2 wrong audio options = other phrases already taught earlier in the *same unit*. No unique distractor content needs authoring — reuse the unit's own vocabulary, which doubles as spaced review.
