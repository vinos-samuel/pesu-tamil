# Curriculum Plan — Spoken Conversational Tamil (v1)

Working draft. Goal: by the end of the available units, a kid can understand and participate in basic real-life spoken exchanges with family — not read/write Tamil, not formal/literary Tamil.

Each unit below = roughly 1–2 weeks of daily 15–20 min sessions. Order is a first draft, not final — sequencing should get validated against what diaspora parents say their kids actually need at home.

## Unit sequence

1. **Greetings & courtesy** — vanakkam, how are you, please/thank you, yes/no, goodbye
2. **Family & kinship** — amma/appa, sibling terms (elder/younger, gender-specific), grandparents, aunts/uncles by relationship type (a genuine Tamil-specific differentiator vs. English's flat "aunt/uncle")
3. **Numbers 1–20** + counting objects
4. **Colors & everyday objects** — around the house, school bag items
5. **Food & mealtime phrases** — "I'm hungry," "tastes good," common dish names, table manners phrases
6. **Body parts & basic health** — "my stomach hurts," "I'm tired"
7. **Action commands & routines** — come here, sit, eat, sleep, wash your hands, brush your teeth — the phrases kids actually hear daily
8. **Questions & short answers** — what, where, who, how much — "where is it," "what is this"
9. **Emotions & feelings** — happy, sad, angry, scared, tired — naming feelings, simple "I feel..." phrases
10. **School & daily schedule** — morning/afternoon/evening, days of the week, "I have school," "I'm going to play"
11. **Festivals & cultural touchpoints** — Pongal, Tamil New Year, temple visit vocabulary, a short well-known song or two lines of a familiar prayer/verse
12. **Putting it together** — short two-line conversations combining prior units (e.g., a grandparent asking "have you eaten?" and a kid responding)

## Content format per phrase/vocab item

For each item, plan to produce:
- Native-speaker audio (adult voice; kid voice for younger-band peer modeling where feasible)
- A short, real-life usage context (not an isolated word — a sentence it's actually used in)
- Romanized Tamil for internal reference only — **not shown to the learner** by default, per the spoken-only/no-script decision, unless UX testing shows kids need a visual anchor
- Tags: age-band suitability, unit, difficulty, "home phrase" vs. "cultural phrase" vs. "core vocab"

## Regional variant note

Spoken Tamil differs meaningfully across India, Sri Lanka, and Singapore/Malaysia communities. Decide per-phrase whether a variant flag/alt-audio option is worth the production cost for v1, or whether v1 picks one baseline (e.g., standard spoken Tamil Nadu Tamil) and defers variants to later.

## Explicit exclusions for v1

- No script/letters, no reading, no writing
- No grammar terminology (no "this is a verb conjugation") — everything taught through usage and repetition, not explanation
- No formal/literary register phrases
